How It Works

Everything you need to know about choosing a project, accessing data, and publishing your work.

The Process

From Course Completion to Portfolio-Ready

1

Complete a Course

The Portfolio Hub is designed for learners who have completed DA-2 (Google Sheets), DA-3 (MySQL), DA-4 (Power BI), or the full Data Analytics programme. You need the course skills to do the project — not the other way around.

2

Browse and Choose a Project

Visit the Explore Projects page. Use the filters to narrow by course, domain (the industry you're interested in), difficulty, or skills you want to practise. Read a few project cards and open the ones that interest you.

3

Understand the Business Challenge

Every project begins with a realistic business problem — not just a technical task. Read the challenge carefully before opening the data. Understanding what the stakeholder needs is half the analytical job.

4

Download the Dataset

Each project links directly to the original data source. Click the dataset link and download the file from the publisher's site (e.g. Kaggle, World Bank, FAO). You'll need a free account on most platforms. The project page tells you exactly what to download and where to find it.

5

Complete the Project

Work through the project tasks at your own pace. Use the project page as your guide — it tells you what to build, what to deliver, and how you'll be assessed. If you get stuck, try the ALX community, Office Hours, or AI assistance (see the Responsible AI guide).

6

Self-Assess with the Rubric

Before you publish, use the assessment rubric on the project page to evaluate your own work honestly. The rubric tells you what good looks like across every dimension. If something doesn't meet the standard, improve it — that's the point.

7

Publish to GitHub

Organise your work in a professional GitHub repository following the structure suggested in the project brief. A good README is essential — it's often the first thing a recruiter or employer reads. See the GitHub Portfolio Guide for a full walkthrough.

8

Showcase on LinkedIn

Share your completed project on LinkedIn. Describe the business problem you solved, what you found, and link to your GitHub repo. The LinkedIn Showcase Guide gives you a post template and tips for communicating your work effectively.

Difficulty Levels Explained

Beginner

Clearly structured problem, one primary dataset, guided tasks, skills closely aligned to the completed course.

Intermediate

Less prescriptive workflow, more complex or imperfect data, stronger emphasis on interpretation and recommendations.

Advanced

Broader business problem, multiple files or tables, ambiguous requirements, high expectations for documentation and executive communication.

These are portfolio opportunities, not graded assignments.

Projects in the Portfolio Hub are completely optional. There is no submission, no grade, and no deadline. The only person you're accountable to is your future employer — and they'll see what you build.
