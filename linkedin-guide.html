LinkedIn Showcase Guide

How to communicate your portfolio project professionally on LinkedIn — and make it visible to the right people.

Why LinkedIn Matters

LinkedIn is where African data professionals are being discovered. Many job offers and recruiter conversations begin with a LinkedIn post or profile view — not a job application. Sharing your portfolio project publicly signals confidence, communication skills, and genuine work. It also helps you build a network of people in your target industry.

The Post Structure That Works

Use this format for every project post:

1. HOOK — Start with the business problem, not the tools. Bad: "I just completed a Power BI dashboard!" Good: "A hospital network was struggling with rising readmission rates..." 2. WHAT YOU DID — Brief, specific, tool-agnostic. "I analysed 3 years of patient admission data, identified the departments with the highest readmission risk, and built an executive dashboard to help leadership act on it." 3. KEY FINDING — One specific, concrete insight. "The cardiology ward had a 30-day readmission rate 2.4x the network average — and it was concentrated in one age group." 4. LINK — Always include the GitHub link. "Full project, SQL scripts, and dashboard: [GitHub link]" 5. TAGS — 5 to 8 relevant hashtags. #DataAnalytics #PowerBI #SQL #HealthcareAnalytics #ALXAfrica #PortfolioProject #DataCareer

Add Your Project to the Featured Section

Don't just post — feature it. On your LinkedIn profile, go to the Featured section, click the + button, and add a link to your GitHub repository. This keeps your best work visible permanently at the top of your profile.

Things to Avoid

  • Don't just say "I built a dashboard." Tell people what problem it solved and what was found.
  • Don't exaggerate. "This analysis could save millions in hospital costs" is not credible from a self-directed portfolio project. Describe what you found, not what it could theoretically achieve.
  • Don't post without a visual. A screenshot of your dashboard or a key chart dramatically increases engagement.
  • Don't forget to acknowledge the data source. "Data from the World Bank Global Findex Database" shows professionalism and intellectual honesty.
  • Don't wait until it's perfect. A completed, well-documented project shared publicly is worth ten perfect projects hidden in a folder.

Engage After Posting

Reply to every comment, including short ones. Ask questions of your own network. Tag the domain you worked in — data people in healthcare, agriculture, and finance do follow these conversations and do reach out.
