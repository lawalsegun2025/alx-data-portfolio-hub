Responsible AI Use

AI is a powerful tool. Used well, it accelerates your learning. Used poorly, it undermines the portfolio you're trying to build.

The Core Principle

You may use AI as a learning and productivity assistant, but not as a substitute for your own analysis and decision-making. Your portfolio should demonstrate what you can do — not what an AI can generate on your behalf.

Employers increasingly know the difference between a learner who used AI to accelerate their work and a learner who used AI to replace it. The former is an asset. The latter is a risk.

✅ Appropriate Uses

  • Asking AI to explain a concept you didn't understand
  • Debugging a SQL query or DAX formula that isn't working
  • Brainstorming analytical questions to ask of your data
  • Reviewing your writing for clarity and grammar
  • Getting a second opinion on your data cleaning approach
  • Understanding what a function or formula does
  • Generating ideas for chart types or dashboard layouts

❌ Inappropriate Uses

  • Asking AI to write your entire analysis or conclusions
  • Copying AI-generated recommendations as your own
  • Submitting AI-generated text in your executive summary without substantial rewriting
  • Uploading sensitive or personal data to public AI tools
  • Using AI-generated visuals or dashboards without building them yourself
  • Relying on AI outputs without verifying them against the data

Why This Matters for Your Portfolio

An AI-generated analysis looks like an AI-generated analysis. Experienced data professionals can spot it quickly — generic phrasing, conclusions that don't connect to the specific numbers, recommendations that could apply to any industry.

A portfolio built on genuine work stands out precisely because it shows your thinking: specific observations, unexpected findings, honest acknowledgement of limitations, and recommendations grounded in what the data actually shows.

That's what gets you hired.

Transparency

If you use AI assistance significantly at any stage of a project, briefly note it in your README. For example: "I used AI assistance to debug my SQL joins and to review the clarity of my executive summary."

This is honest, professional, and increasingly expected. It does not disqualify the work — it contextualises it.

Verification is Your Responsibility

AI tools can and do produce incorrect analysis, wrong formulas, and plausible-sounding nonsense. Every output you use from an AI tool must be verified against your actual data. If you can't verify it, don't use it.

Your name is on the work. The responsibility is yours.
